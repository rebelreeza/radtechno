Ideal State vs. Likely Current State

The goal isn't a perfect ERP - it's a trusted one. These are the eight areas where "trust" either exists or breaks down, and what each looks like when it's working versus when it isn't.

Area Ideal State Broken State Current State
Inventory accuracy Odoo is the single number. Shopify displays what Odoo says. No reconciliation needed. Shopify and Odoo show different counts. Team manually checks which is right before making decisions.
Order flow Every Shopify order appears in Odoo within minutes. Fulfillment state stays in sync automatically. Orders appear late or with wrong status. Someone checks for stuck orders daily and manually fixes them.
Financial close Month-end close takes 1-2 days. Revenue in Odoo matches Shopify payouts with explainable variance only. Month-end takes a week. Finance exports from Shopify, exports from Odoo, reconciles in Excel.
Purchasing Purchase orders in Odoo flow to vendor, goods receipt auto-updates inventory, landed cost captured cleanly. POs managed in email or spreadsheet. Goods receipt entered manually. Landed cost guessed or ignored.
Reporting Leadership pulls one number. It's right. No caveat of "well, that's the Shopify number, Odoo says something different." Every report comes with an asterisk. Leadership has learned not to trust it and asks for manual confirmation.
Multi-store management 70 stores in one Odoo instance. Inventory per store, roll-up reporting across all. One place to look. Some stores share an Odoo company, others are siloed. Cross-store reporting requires manual aggregation.
Data discipline Required fields enforced. Naming conventions followed. No notes fields being used as a database. Free-text fields everywhere. Same vendor entered three different ways. Reports can't aggregate correctly.
Failure visibility When the Shopify sync fails, someone gets a Slack alert within minutes. Root cause is logged. Sync fails silently. Discovered days later when inventory is wrong and nobody knows why.
Shopify–Odoo Flow

How Data Moves Between Shopify and Odoo

The connector between Shopify and Odoo is the most load-bearing piece of the entire stack. Everything downstream - inventory accuracy, financial close, reporting - depends on it behaving correctly under all conditions, not just the happy path.

The core sync loop

Shopify is the customer-facing system of record for orders, products, and payments. Odoo is the operational and financial system of record. The connector's job is to translate between them in near-real-time. In practice, this means: Shopify orders become Odoo Sales Orders, product catalog changes flow both directions, inventory levels push from Odoo to Shopify, and fulfillment status syncs back. When this loop is tight and reliable, everything else works. When it isn't, the failure ripples everywhere.

What Odoo does with a Shopify order

An order paid in Shopify syncs into Odoo as a Sales Order. Odoo auto-generates an invoice and marks it paid immediately - it's an accounting record, not a customer document. The connector then needs to handle the fulfillment lifecycle: pick, pack, ship, delivered. Each state change in Shopify should update Odoo, and vice versa. Where most connectors fall short is the full returns and refund cycle: a return in Shopify needs to create a credit note in Odoo, restock inventory, and close the loop on the original invoice cleanly.

Inventory: the most contested field

Inventory is the single most common source of Shopify-Odoo disagreement. Both systems want to be authoritative, and connectors often let each win in different scenarios - creating drift. The only sustainable rule: Odoo is authoritative, Shopify is display-only. That means inventory edits happen in Odoo, and the connector pushes them out. Direct edits in Shopify need to be locked down for staff. Without this rule enforced, you get divergence that compounds over time and is very hard to unwind.

Edge cases that expose implementation quality

Chargebacks are the hardest: the order is already shipped and invoiced as paid in Odoo when the bank claws the cash back. Shopify Payments holds (fraud flags) create the same mismatch. Partial refunds, split fulfillments, and creator-specific discount logic are other common failure modes. A connector that handles the happy path is table stakes. The question worth asking is which of these edge cases are currently handled automatically versus reconciled manually - and how often.

Diagnostics

Common Failure Modes

These are the patterns that cause trust in an ERP to collapse - and the fixes are usually more organizational than technical.

Silent sync failures

The Shopify connector runs on a schedule or webhook. When it fails, there's often no alert. Inventory drifts, orders go missing, nobody notices until the problem has compounded.

Fix: monitoring on every sync run, Slack alert on failure, retry logic with logging. Failure should be loud, not silent.

No source-of-truth rule

Both Shopify and Odoo think they're authoritative for inventory. When they disagree, the connector picks one - often arbitrarily, because nobody documented the rule at implementation.

Fix: establish and enforce explicitly - Odoo is authoritative, Shopify is display-only. Lock inventory editing in Shopify for staff.

Bypassing Odoo "just this once"

Someone adjusts inventory directly in Shopify to solve an immediate problem. Odoo and Shopify are now out of sync. This becomes habitual when people don't trust Odoo - which makes them trust it even less.

Fix: all inventory adjustments through Odoo only. Document the rule, enforce it, and make Odoo the easier path.

Chart of accounts misconfiguration

Set up wrong at implementation and never corrected. Revenue hits the wrong account, reports don't map to how the business actually works, and finance has learned to distrust anything Odoo produces.

Fix: reconcile with finance, remap accounts, correct historical entries where necessary. This is a one-time fix with compounding returns.

Implementation data never cleaned

Vendors entered multiple ways, product names inconsistent, missing required fields. Reports that should aggregate can't, because the underlying records don't share a common key.

Fix: data quality sprint before any new reporting investment. Reports are only as good as the data behind them.

No implementation documentation

The person who made the configuration decisions is gone. Why certain fields map where they do, what custom logic exists, what was intentional vs. temporary - that context exists only in someone's memory.

Fix: document the current state as-found before touching anything. This becomes the baseline for all future decisions.
Discovery

Questions That Provide Initial Insight

These are the questions that help uncover diagnostic understanding of the current Odoo implementation.

  • 01
    Walk me through where Odoo is and isn't working today - in your own words, not the polished version.
  • 02
    When you think about what's frustrating - is it that you can't trust what the system shows, or that it's hard for the team to actually use, or both?
  • 03
    Where do people go when they can't trust what Odoo shows?
  • 04
    What does a month-end close actually look like right now, step by step?
  • 05
    How are returns handled - does that flow back into Odoo automatically, or is it a manual process?
  • 06
    How are chargebacks handled - does Odoo get updated, or does finance deal with that separately?
  • 07
    When the Shopify sync fails, how does anyone find out?
  • 08
    Is there anything that was configured during implementation that you now know was the wrong call?
  • 09
    Anything not covered here that's worth sharing?
