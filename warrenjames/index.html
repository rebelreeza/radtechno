Current State

What I Think Is True Right Now

Based on everything I've learned so far, here's my honest read of where things stand. I'm sharing this because I want you to correct me where I'm wrong - I'd rather surface a wrong assumption now than act on it later.

Odoo

Functional but not trusted

The system is running, but the team is probably maintaining parallel spreadsheets for anything important. The Shopify connector is often where trust breaks down in setups like this - silent sync failures that go unnoticed until the numbers don't match.

Shopify

70 stores, unknown consistency

Each store likely has its own app stack, its own structure, and its own workarounds. There's probably significant overlap in paid tooling and significant variation in how drops are coordinated. The full picture doesn't exist anywhere yet.

Knowledge

Concentrated risk

The decisions made during the Odoo implementation - which fields map where, why certain things were configured a certain way - are likely not well documented. Whether that was an implementation partner, an internal resource, or some combination, the reasoning behind key configuration choices rarely survives the handoff.

Automation

High potential, low current baseline

The team is probably doing manually what could be automated - request triage, order routing, campaign coordination across stores. That's not a criticism - it's where some of the earliest leverage likely lives, and the tooling to address it is relatively lightweight.

The Real Risk

Speed of the business outpacing the infrastructure holding it together

Creators move at the speed of social media. A drop that goes viral on a Tuesday needs the ops infrastructure to handle it by Wednesday. Right now, the reliability of that infrastructure is unclear - and at 70 stores, a systemic failure isn't a one-store problem.

The Plan

Four Phases - One Direction

Stabilize the foundation fast, build leverage into it, then compound. The sequencing is intentional - later phases depend on earlier ones being solid.

Days 1 - 30 · Phase 1
Orient - Understand before acting
The output of month one is a ranked list of what's actually broken - not a change log. I won't fix anything that isn't causing live operational damage.
  • Sit with ops, finance, and merch - ask "where do you go when you can't trust Odoo?"
  • Pull a sample of orders and trace them end-to-end through Shopify and Odoo - find where states diverge
  • Map every manual reconciliation happening at month-end
  • Full Shopify audit: plan types, app overlap, store structure consistency, sync behavior
  • SaaS and vendor audit - most companies this size carry $15-40K/month in redundant tooling
  • Ship one high-visibility AI automation early to prove the pattern - not to impress, to establish the template
Months 2 - 3 · Phase 2
Stabilize - Make Odoo trustworthy
This is the unglamorous work that makes everything else possible. The goal: a week goes by with no "the inventory numbers are wrong" conversation.
  • Fix the Shopify sync - monitoring, alerting, and conflict resolution documented
  • Clean the data sources that are producing unreliable reports (not the reports themselves)
  • Automate the 3 most painful manual handoffs identified in Phase 1
  • Stand up the data pipeline to BigQuery - so Phase 3 has clean data to work with
Months 4 - 6 · Phase 3
Leverage - Build on a stable base
With a reliable foundation, shift from fixing to building. AI workflows embedded in how teams actually operate, not just demos.
  • AI embedded in every team's workflow - ops triage, creative briefs, inventory forecasting
  • Campaign tracking discipline across all 70 stores - UTM tagging, Shopify Flow, consistent attribution
  • Creator store "fingerprints" - encode what makes each store's audience unique as structured data
  • Shopify store consolidation where it makes sense - fewer redundant apps, consistent drop infrastructure
Months 7 - 9 · Phase 4
Compound - Build competitive advantage
The things in this phase require clean data and working infrastructure underneath them - which is why the earlier phases have to come first.
  • Cross-store campaign intelligence - when Store A has a breakout drop, automatically identify which other stores have the most similar audience profile and generate tailored briefs
  • Purchasing leverage - 15 stores buying from the same manufacturer should negotiate as one buyer
  • Internal AI tools built on top of clean data and the workflow patterns established in Phase 3
Bigger Picture

Where This Leads

The 6-9 month work is about stabilizing the foundation. But the reason to build it carefully is what it unlocks. These are the outcomes that justify the investment - none of them are possible without clean data and reliable infrastructure underneath.

Flagship Opportunity

Cross-Store Campaign Pattern Matching

WJ manages ~70 creator stores - more launch data than any individual creator will accumulate on their own. After every successful drop, leverage agentic AI to extract what drove performance: audience trust index, product category, price band, scarcity mechanics, sell-through velocity. When Store A has a breakout launch, the system could identify the 4-5 stores with the most similar fingerprint and generate tailored campaign briefs for each. Over time this could become a proprietary playbook library that compounds with every new launch - the kind of institutional intelligence that's hard to replicate without the same data history.

New Revenue Line

Creator-Owned Apps

Hank Green's Focus Friend hit #1 on the App Store in 2025 with a tiny team - the distribution came entirely from his audience, not paid acquisition. WJ's creator roster has the same distribution potential, and most creators have no product or tech expertise to act on it. That gap could be a material opportunity enabled by a tech org with app-building capability and product judgment.

Operations

Launch Command Center

Coordinating a drop across inventory, fulfillment, Shopify, and creator comms is typically a manual high-stress event. With the right automation layer, that could look more like a pre-staged, orchestrated workflow - inventory positioned ahead of time, Launchpad queued, notifications handled automatically.

Finance

Real-Time Portfolio View

With clean Odoo data flowing into BigQuery, a live portfolio view across all 70 stores - revenue, inventory, margin - becomes achievable without anyone manually pulling and reconciling exports.
