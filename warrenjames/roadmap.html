The governing constraint: Warren James runs ~70 Shopify stores for creators who move at the speed of social media. That means the cost of being slow is real revenue. Every decision in this roadmap is made through that lens - what unblocks the business fastest vs. what's a nice-to-have that can wait.
Phase 1

Orient & Listen - Don't Touch, Don't Fix

Days 1 – 30
The biggest mistake in this role would be coming in with answers before understanding the problem. The first 30 days are an audit, not a build sprint. My output is a ranked list of what's actually broken and what the team has been working around - not a change log.
Primary mandate
Understand before acting
ERP

Map the Real State of Odoo

Not what it's supposed to do - what it actually does. Where do people not trust it? Where are they maintaining a parallel spreadsheet? What breaks every week?
  • Sit with each team that touches Odoo (ops, finance, merch) - ask "where do you go when you can't trust this?"
  • Pull a sample of orders and trace them end-to-end through both Shopify and Odoo - find where the states diverge
  • Map every manual reconciliation that happens at month-end
  • Document every Odoo workaround currently in production
Decision rule: Don't fix anything in Phase 1 unless it's causing live operational damage (oversells, wrong invoices going out). Everything else goes on the list.
Shopify

Inventory the Shopify Reality

~70 stores. Are they all on the same plan, under the same org, with consistent structure? The answer is probably no - and I need to understand exactly what I've inherited.
  • Audit which stores are on Plus vs standard - document what each is actually using
  • Map all active third-party apps across all stores and their cost
  • Identify which stores share infrastructure vs. which are completely siloed
  • Find out where Launchpad is used and how drops are currently coordinated
  • Understand the Shopify ↔ Odoo sync: direction, frequency, failure behavior
Decision rule: Consolidation decisions (plan changes, org structure) belong in Phase 2. Phase 1 is observation only.
AI / Automation

Find the 3 Most Painful Manual Handoffs

The mandate is to reduce manual handoffs. I need to find the ones actually killing productivity - not the ones that feel interesting to automate.
  • Shadow one person in each function for half a day - ops, finance, creative/merch, creator success
  • Look for copy-paste chains: where does someone take data from system A and type it into system B?
  • Ask: "What do you do every Monday/first-of-month that you wish you didn't have to?"
  • Rank by: time cost × frequency × error rate
Governance

SaaS & Vendor Audit

Most companies this size carry $15–40K/month in redundant or abandoned SaaS. I need a full picture of what's in the building before making any tooling decisions.
  • Pull all active subscriptions from billing (credit cards, corporate accounts)
  • Map who uses what and what they'd lose if it went away
  • Identify overlapping tools (multiple project managers, duplicate communication apps)
  • Flag anything with no clear owner - that's a security risk
  • Note contract renewal dates - any coming up in the next 90 days
Quick win opportunity: Killing 2–3 unused subscriptions in week 2 is a visible win that costs zero credibility and banks goodwill.
Action

The One Thing I Build in Month 1

One high-visibility, low-risk AI automation ships in week 2 - not to impress, but to prove the pattern. I'll pick the highest-signal handoff from the audit and build it before anyone asks. The specific workflow depends on what the audit surfaces, but the profile is consistent: Slack or Asana as the trigger, Claude as the reasoning layer, n8n as the orchestration engine. Builds in hours, runs forever, teams feel it immediately.
  • Most likely candidate: inbound ops request triage - messages that are currently handled manually get classified, routed, and ticketed automatically
  • Add memory so the agent can reference prior tickets and update rather than duplicate
  • Document it so ops can modify routing rules themselves - this is the template for every automation that follows
  • Measure usage weekly - this is the first data point for "AI used weekly across teams"
Phase 2

Stabilize - Make Odoo Trustworthy

Months 2 – 3
Phase 2 is the unglamorous work that makes everything else possible. Odoo can't feel stable until the data going in is clean and the sync to Shopify is reliable. This is the work most candidates skip - and why trust in the system erodes over time.
Primary mandate
One source of truth
ERP

Fix the Shopify ↔ Odoo Sync

This is almost certainly the root cause of every "the numbers don't match" conversation. The fix isn't glamorous but it's the highest-leverage thing in the whole roadmap.
  • Identify all failure modes in the current sync - silent failures, conflict resolution, rate limits
  • Add monitoring so failures are visible immediately instead of discovered days later
  • Define the conflict resolution rule: when Shopify and Odoo disagree on inventory, which wins and why?
  • Test the sync under campaign load - what happens when 70 stores get a surge simultaneously?
  • Document it so anyone on the team can debug it without me
Success signal: A week goes by with no "the inventory numbers are wrong" conversation. That's the signal Phase 2 is done.
ERP

Close the Top 3 Data Model Gaps

Based on Phase 1 audit. These are the specific fields, records, or structures that are producing unreliable reports. Fix the sources, not the outputs.
  • Establish data entry standards: required fields, dropdown over free text, naming conventions
  • Deduplicate critical records - vendors, products, contacts - starting with the ones impacting finance
  • Move any load-bearing spreadsheet data into Odoo with proper structure
  • Close the "notes field as structured data" problem - create proper metafields for recurring use cases
AI / Automation

Ship the 3 Highest-ROI Automations

From the Phase 1 pain map. Pick the handoffs that are most frequent, most error-prone, and most manual. Build them with n8n. Measure before and after.
  • Likely candidate: New creator onboarding → database entry → Asana kickoff → Slack notification (eliminate the Google Sheet)
  • Likely candidate: Order anomaly detection - flag orders where Shopify and Odoo state diverges for more than X minutes
  • Likely candidate: Campaign launch checklist - when a drop is scheduled, auto-prep the checklist across Asana, notify the creator team, confirm inventory is staged
  • Each automation gets a Slack alert on failure so it never silently breaks
Data

Stand Up the Reporting Foundation

Leadership shouldn't be reconciling spreadsheets to get a Monday morning number. Build the plumbing now so the dashboard in Phase 3 actually shows the right thing.
  • Set up BigQuery (or equivalent) - start syncing Shopify and Odoo via Fivetran or Airbyte
  • Define the 5 metrics the CEO looks at every week - build those first, nothing else
  • Connect Looker Studio or Metabase for zero-code access by non-technical users
  • Validate outputs against source systems before sharing - don't show the CEO a dashboard that's wrong
Do NOT: Build 40 dashboards before the 5 core metrics are trusted. Breadth before trust is how dashboards get ignored.
Phase 3

Build for Leverage - AI Across Teams

Months 4 – 6
The foundation is stable. Now I build what actually changes how the business operates. This is where AI stops being a demo and starts being infrastructure - workflows that run on their own, data that answers questions without someone pulling it, teams that move faster because the tools move with them.
Primary mandate
Teams self-serve
AI / Automation

AI Workflows Running in Every Team

The goal is AI embedded in how every team operates - not just workflows I built, but workflows teams own. Phase 3 is about enabling that through tools, templates, and internal training.
  • Ops team: AI-powered inbound message triage with memory (built on Phase 1 foundation, now mature)
  • Finance: Month-end reconciliation assistant - flags discrepancies, drafts summaries, suggests reclassifications
  • Marketing/creator team: Campaign brief generator - pull a creator's performance history, generate a tailored launch strategy doc
  • Creator success: Automated weekly store performance digest per creator - no one manually compiling reports
  • Run an internal "AI office hours" session - teach teams to build simple workflows themselves using n8n or Make
Shopify

Shopify Infrastructure Consolidation

Based on Phase 1 audit findings. This is about removing the debt - redundant apps, inconsistent store structures, the manual coordination that happens at launch time.
  • Standardize app stack across stores - one approved set, remove redundant installs
  • Launchpad deployment: every coordinated creator drop uses Launchpad, not manual "everyone go live at 9am"
  • Shopify Flow for cross-store order tagging - automated campaign attribution starts now
  • Evaluate Shopify Plus consolidation ROI if stores are on split plans - present the math with a clear recommendation
Data

Campaign Intelligence Layer

The strategic AI use case - post-campaign analysis that extracts what drove success and matches it to similar stores. This is the work that becomes a moat.
  • Add campaign tracking to Shopify orders via UTM discipline + Shopify Flow tagging - mandatory from day one of Phase 3
  • Build the campaign_performance table in BigQuery - one row per campaign per store
  • First post-campaign debrief with AI synthesis: what drove performance, what the sell-through curve looked like, what to do differently
  • Creator store fingerprint: encode success attributes per store so cross-store matching becomes possible in Phase 4
Why Phase 3, not earlier: Two months of clean data need to flow into BigQuery before campaign analytics mean anything. Starting the pipeline in Phase 2 means Phase 3 has data to work with.
Governance

Access Management & Offboarding Protocol

70 stores, fast-growing company, already had a key tech person leave. The offboarding risk is real - former employee access across 70 Shopify stores is a liability. Fix it before the next departure.
  • Audit current Shopify admin access - every account across every store
  • Implement SSO where possible - Google Workspace as the identity layer
  • Offboarding checklist that covers all systems in under 30 minutes
  • Quarterly access review - not a bureaucratic process, a 2-hour audit once per quarter
Phase 4

Compound - Strategic Intelligence

Months 7 – 9
By Month 7, the foundation is solid, the workflows are running, and the data is clean and accumulating. Phase 4 is where I shift from building infrastructure to building competitive advantage. The things in this phase only work because of everything that came before.
Primary mandate
Business intelligence moat
AI / Automation

Cross-Store Campaign Pattern Matching

The flagship AI use case. A successful campaign in Store A auto-generates a brief for the 5 stores with the most similar audience profiles. This is institutional knowledge the business owns and competitors can't buy.
  • Cross-store attribute similarity engine - which stores share audience type, price band, category, trust index?
  • Post-campaign AI debrief: structured analysis of what drove success, encoded into store fingerprint
  • Proactive playbook generation: "Store 12 just had a breakout drop - here are the 4 stores most likely to replicate it and why"
  • Inputs: BigQuery campaign data + social analytics (engagement rate, audience demo) from Instagram/TikTok API
Why this only works now: This requires 6+ months of tagged campaign data and 2+ months of store fingerprint profiles before similarity matching is meaningful. Attempting it in Month 2 produces noise, not insight.
Data

CEO Operating Dashboard

Not 40 dashboards. One view the CEO opens every Monday that answers: how is the portfolio doing, where are the problems, and what needs a decision today.
  • Portfolio GMV vs. prior week, month, same period last year - across all stores
  • Top 5 and bottom 5 performing stores - ranked by this week's sell-through rate
  • Active campaigns: what's live, what's closing, what's pending
  • Odoo health signal: sync status, any anomalies flagged in the last 7 days
  • AI-generated weekly narrative: "The story of last week in 3 bullets" - synthesized from the data automatically
AI / Automation

Creator Commerce Coach

Give creators access to AI-powered insights about their own store that they couldn't get anywhere else. This is the retention play - leaving Warren James means losing this capability.
  • Creator-facing performance digest: "Here's what worked in your last launch and why"
  • Pre-launch recommendations: "Based on your audience, these product types and price points have the highest sell-through probability"
  • Benchmark visibility: "Your engagement-to-conversion rate is in the top quartile of stores your size"
  • Delivered via Slack or a lightweight web view - no new app required
ERP Data

Vendor Intelligence & Procurement Optimization

With clean cross-store data, purchasing leverage that was invisible when each store was siloed becomes actionable. 15 stores buying from the same manufacturer should negotiate as one buyer.
  • Map shared vendor relationships across all 70 stores - total spend per vendor in Odoo
  • Identify top 10 vendors by total WJ portfolio spend - present consolidated leverage case
  • Automate reorder alerts: when any store's stock for a shared SKU-category drops below threshold, flag it
  • Lead time intelligence: which vendors deliver on time, which create inventory gaps during launches

Strong Intuition: What to Build Now vs Later

Build now - months 1–3

Shopify ↔ Odoo sync monitoring (day one priority)
Root cause of leadership distrust. Everything else depends on this being reliable.
BigQuery pipeline (Shopify + Odoo → warehouse)
Data needs to accumulate before it's useful. Start the clock in month 2.
SaaS audit and consolidation
Free money, zero risk. Do it week 2.
3 highest-frequency manual handoff automations
Operationally visible, teams feel the difference immediately.
Odoo data standards (required fields, naming, no free-text metafields)
Cheap to set, expensive to fix later. Establish before more data goes in dirty.

Build later - months 4–9

Cross-store campaign pattern matching
Requires 6 months of clean campaign data. Premature before that.
Creator Commerce Coach (creator-facing AI)
Only valuable once the underlying store data is trustworthy and structured.
Shopify Plus - gap analysis and consideration
Plus unlocks B2B, Checkout Extensibility, and multi-store admin. At ~$2k/month per store it's a significant cost decision. Worth a structured evaluation after the store audit - not a default assumption.
Checkout Extensibility / Shopify Functions
High-leverage but requires Shopify Plus. Only relevant after a deliberate decision to upgrade - don't design for features we don't have access to yet.
Custom internal tooling (Supabase-backed apps)
Only if Airtable genuinely can't do the job. Don't build what can be configured.
Headless / Hydrogen storefronts
Not on the roadmap unless a specific store's revenue clearly justifies the engineering overhead. Shopify's native storefront is not the bottleneck.

Vision Horizon - Where This Leads

The 6–9 month roadmap is about stabilizing the foundation and proving the pattern. But the reason to build it carefully is what it unlocks after. Every use case below is impossible without clean data, a reliable sync layer, and AI workflows already embedded in how teams operate. The foundation isn't the goal - it's the prerequisite. These are the outcomes that justify it.

Campaign Intelligence · Flagship

Cross-Store Campaign Pattern Matching

After every successful launch, an AI agent extracts the characteristics that drove performance - audience trust index, product category, price band, scarcity mechanics, sell-through velocity - and encodes them as a store fingerprint. When Store A has a breakout drop, the system automatically identifies the 4–5 stores with the most similar profiles and generates a tailored campaign brief for each. Over time this becomes a proprietary playbook library that no competitor can replicate without the same data history. Institutional knowledge that compounds.

Every successful launch teaches the whole portfolio. One win becomes five.
Creator Intelligence

New Creator Fit Predictor

Signing a new creator partnership is one of the most capital-intensive decisions Warren James makes. Today it's largely qualitative. With two years of store fingerprint data, it becomes predictive - score any prospective creator against the audience profiles and product categories where WJ consistently wins, and rank them by expected Year 1 GMV before a single dollar is committed.

Data-driven creator acquisition. Gut feel replaced with ranked probability.
Campaign Intelligence

Optimal Launch Timing Engine

Patterns in campaign data reveal which days, times, and seasonal windows produce the highest sell-through for each store's audience type. The system stops being reactive - instead of "when does the creator want to post?" it becomes "here's the highest-probability launch window for this creator's audience in Q4, based on 18 months of their performance data."

Higher sell-through on every launch. No more guessing posting windows.
Creator Growth

Creator Commerce Coach

Every creator gets an AI-powered weekly digest of their store performance - sell-through trends, audience behavior, what's working and why - framed as actionable recommendations, not raw data. Delivered via Slack or a lightweight web view. The strategic implication: leaving Warren James means losing this capability. It turns the partnership into infrastructure.

Creator retention tool. WJ becomes irreplaceable, not just convenient.
Product Intelligence

Launch Inventory Optimizer

Consistently understocking a launch leaves revenue on the table. Overstocking means sitting on margin-eroding inventory. With historical sell-through velocity by store, category, and campaign type, the system recommends launch inventory quantities - and flags when a planned allocation looks mismatched against the store's historical demand curve.

Less stockout. Less dead inventory. Better margin on every launch.
Revenue Intelligence

Revenue Anomaly Explainer

When revenue spikes or drops unexpectedly, someone currently has to manually investigate across Shopify, Odoo, and social - correlating an order surge with a post, a dip with a sync failure, a return spike with a product quality issue. The AI agent does this automatically: detects the anomaly, traces it to the most likely cause, and surfaces the explanation before anyone asks.

CEO Monday meeting starts with explanations, not questions.
Ops Intelligence

Portfolio Vendor Leverage

Fifteen stores buying from the same manufacturer appear as fifteen separate relationships - each negotiating independently, each blind to the others. With cross-store vendor data consolidated in one layer, Warren James sees its true aggregate spend per supplier. That's the basis for consolidating purchasing power, negotiating better terms, and identifying which vendors are creating inventory risk across multiple stores simultaneously.

Buying power of a portfolio, not 70 individual stores.
Audience Intelligence

Cross-Store Buyer Identity Graph

A customer who bought from Creator A's store and Creator B's store is a high-value repeat buyer across the WJ portfolio - but if each store is siloed they're invisible as such. Connecting buyer identities across stores (via email match) reveals the true lifetime value of the WJ audience, which creators share the most loyal overlap, and where cross-promotion is most likely to convert.

The WJ audience is worth more than the sum of 70 individual stores.
Why none of this works without the foundation: Clean campaign tagging starts in Phase 3. Store fingerprints require 6+ months of tagged data. Cross-store buyer identity requires consistent customer records in Odoo. Vendor leverage requires unified spend data in BigQuery. The 6–9 month roadmap isn't a delay before the interesting work - it's what makes the interesting work possible.
